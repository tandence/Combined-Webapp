Sleep

Sleep plays an important role in how your body recovers, how you feel, and how you manage pain day to day.

Why sleep matters

Sleep affects how you feel both physically and emotionally.

Poor sleep can increase pain sensitivity, reduce energy levels and make it harder to cope day to day.

Improving sleep can help support your overall wellbeing and how manageable pain feels.

Sleep and pain are linked

Pain can make it more difficult to sleep, and poor sleep can make pain feel worse.

This can sometimes create a cycle where sleep and pain affect each other.

Understanding this link can help you focus on small changes that support both sleep and pain management.

Sleep is not always perfect

It is common to have disrupted or poor sleep when living with persistent pain.

Trying to achieve “perfect sleep” can sometimes increase pressure and make sleep feel more difficult.

Focusing on small, consistent habits can be more helpful over time.

Things that may help

  • Keep a regular wake-up time, even after a poor night
  • Develop a wind-down routine before bed
  • Limit screen use in the evening
  • If unable to sleep, get up and return when sleepy
  • Keep your bedroom comfortable, cool and dark

Small changes, practiced regularly, can help improve sleep patterns over time.

Building a routine

Having a regular sleep routine can help your body know when it is time to rest.

Going to bed and waking up at similar times each day can support a more consistent sleep pattern.

It may take time to see changes, but routine can make a difference over time.

A balanced approach

Improving sleep does not happen all at once, and some nights may feel more difficult than others.

Sleep is one part of the wider Pain Management Jigsaw, working alongside movement, thoughts and other strategies.

Small, steady changes can help support better sleep over time.
