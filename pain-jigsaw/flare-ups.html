Flare-Ups

Flare-ups are a common part of living with persistent pain. They can feel difficult at the time, but they do not usually mean harm or damage.

Understanding flare-ups

Flare-ups are a temporary increase in pain. They can happen even when you are doing all the right things.

This can feel frustrating or worrying, but it does not usually mean that your condition has changed or that damage is being done.

Understanding this can help reduce worry and support you to respond in a more helpful way.

Why flare-ups happen

Flare-ups can be influenced by a number of factors, often in combination.

  • Doing more activity than usual
  • Stress or emotional strain
  • Poor sleep or fatigue
  • Changes in routine or environment

Pain is influenced by many different factors, so flare-ups are rarely caused by just one thing.

What a flare-up does not mean

  • It does not mean you have caused damage
  • It does not mean you have gone backwards
  • It does not mean your progress has been lost

Flare-ups are a normal part of persistent pain and do not undo the progress you have made.

What can help during a flare-up

  • Reduce activity slightly rather than stopping completely
  • Use pacing strategies to stay steady
  • Take regular short breaks
  • Use relaxation or calming techniques

Small adjustments can help you stay active without making symptoms worse.

Planning for a flare-up

Having a simple plan can help you feel more prepared and reduce worry if a flare-up happens.

  • Know what tends to help you
  • Keep helpful tools nearby
  • Plan gentle activities for more difficult days

Returning to activity

As a flare-up settles, it can be helpful to gradually return to your usual activities.

Starting small and building up again can help you regain confidence and reduce the risk of further flare-ups.

A balanced approach

Flare-ups are a normal part of living with persistent pain.

Learning how to respond to them can help reduce their impact and make them feel more manageable over time.

Like the other pieces of the Pain Management Jigsaw, managing flare-ups works best alongside pacing, movement and managing thoughts and emotions.
