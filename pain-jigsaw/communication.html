Communication

Talking openly about your pain can help others understand your experience and support you in ways that are helpful to you.

Why communication matters

Pain is not always visible, and it can be difficult for others to understand how it affects you.

This can sometimes lead to feeling misunderstood or unsupported.

Sharing your experience can help others better understand your needs and support you in your daily life.

Talking about pain can feel difficult

Many people find it hard to talk about pain, especially if they feel worried about how others may respond.

You may worry about not being understood, being judged, or not wanting to burden others.

These concerns are common and completely understandable.

Talking to others

  • Be open about how pain affects you
  • Explain what helps and what does not
  • Share your needs and limits
  • Let others know how they can support you

Clear communication can help reduce misunderstandings and make support more effective.

Talking to healthcare professionals

  • Describe how pain affects your daily life
  • Ask questions if you are unsure
  • Share any concerns about treatment or support
  • Work together to find approaches that suit you

You are an important part of your care, and your experience helps guide the support you receive.

Common challenges

It can be difficult to talk about pain, especially if you feel misunderstood or unsure how others may respond.

Communication can take time and practice, and it is normal not to find it easy at first.

Taking small steps can help build confidence over time.

Building confidence

Like other skills, communication improves with practice.

Starting with small conversations and building up over time can help you feel more confident.

You do not need to explain everything at once — sharing a little at a time can still make a difference.

A balanced approach

Communication is one part of the wider Pain Management Jigsaw.

It works alongside other approaches such as managing thoughts, pacing activity and setting goals.

Feeling understood and supported can make it easier to live well with pain.
