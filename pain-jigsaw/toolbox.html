Self-Management Toolbox

Everyone’s experience of pain is different. Developing a range of tools can help you manage pain in different situations and feel more in control day to day.

Building your toolbox

Everyone’s experience of pain is different, and what works for one person may not work for another.

Many people living with persistent pain have already tried a range of treatments and approaches. Even with effort, pain can sometimes continue — this does not mean you have done anything wrong.

Having a range of tools means you can choose what helps in different situations.

Physical tools

  • Heat or cold packs
  • Topical gels or patches
  • TENS machines
  • Support cushions or aids

These tools can help ease symptoms and provide short-term relief.

Daily strategies

  • Using pacing and activity management
  • Planning your day
  • Balancing activity and rest

These strategies can help you stay active without overdoing things and support steadier symptoms over time.

Wellbeing tools

  • Relaxation techniques
  • Mindfulness exercises
  • Managing stress and emotions

Supporting your emotional wellbeing can help reduce tension and improve how pain is experienced.

How these tools work together

Pain is influenced by many different factors, including activity, sleep, thoughts, emotions and environment.

Using a range of tools together can often be more effective than relying on just one approach.

You can think of your toolbox as part of the wider Pain Management Jigsaw — different pieces working together to support you.

Finding what works for you

  • Try different tools and techniques
  • Keep track of what helps
  • Use different tools for different situations

Over time, you can build a personalised toolbox that works for your daily life.

A balanced approach

There is no single solution for managing persistent pain. Most people find that combining different approaches works best.

Small, gradual changes over time can make a meaningful difference to how you feel and what you are able to do.
