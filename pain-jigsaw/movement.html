Movement

Gentle, regular movement can help you stay active, build confidence and support how your body feels over time.

Why movement matters

When you are in pain, it can feel natural to avoid movement. However, doing less over time can reduce strength, flexibility and confidence.

Persistent pain can make the body and nervous system more sensitive, meaning movement may feel more difficult or uncomfortable.

Gentle, regular movement can help your body adapt, improve confidence and support your overall wellbeing.

Changing how you think about movement

It is common to worry that movement might cause harm or make pain worse.

While some discomfort can be part of getting moving again, it does not always mean damage is being done.

Understanding this can help you begin to move with more confidence.

Starting safely

  • Begin with small, manageable amounts of activity
  • Choose activities that feel comfortable and achievable
  • Build up gradually over time
  • Be guided by what feels right for you

Starting small can help your body and confidence build steadily.

Types of movement

Different types of movement can support your body in different ways:

  • Walking or gentle outdoor activity
  • Stretching and flexibility exercises
  • Yoga, pilates or tai chi
  • Chair-based or low-impact exercise

It is less about choosing the “right” activity, and more about finding something you can do regularly.

Building confidence

It is normal to feel unsure about movement when you are in pain.

Over time, with gradual practice, you can build confidence and find activities that work for you.

  • Focus on consistency rather than intensity
  • Avoid pushing through severe pain
  • Use pacing strategies to support your activity

Building up gradually

When activity has reduced over time, it is normal for strength, stamina and confidence to decrease.

Building up gradually allows your body and nervous system to adjust at a steady pace.

Small increases over time can make a meaningful difference to what you are able to do.

A balanced approach

Some days may be easier than others when it comes to movement.

Finding a balance between activity and rest can help you stay active without increasing symptoms too much.

Movement is one part of the wider Pain Management Jigsaw, working alongside sleep, thoughts, support and other strategies.
