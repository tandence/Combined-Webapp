Relaxation and Mindfulness

Relaxation and mindfulness can help calm your body and mind, supporting your nervous system and helping you respond differently to pain.

Why it can help

Living with persistent pain can increase stress, tension and worry. This can make the nervous system more sensitive and increase how strongly pain is felt.

Learning ways to relax can help calm the body and mind, supporting your system to settle and feel more manageable.

How it works

Pain is influenced by both the body and the brain. When the body is tense or stressed, pain signals can feel stronger.

Relaxation and mindfulness techniques help reduce tension and bring your attention back to the present moment.

Over time, this can help you feel more in control and reduce the impact of pain on daily life.

Relaxation

Relaxation techniques focus on reducing physical tension in the body.

  • Breathing exercises
  • Muscle relaxation
  • Guided relaxation

These techniques can help slow your breathing, reduce muscle tension and support your body to feel calmer.

Mindfulness

Mindfulness involves focusing on the present moment, without judgement.

It can help you notice thoughts, feelings and sensations without becoming overwhelmed by them.

Many people find this helps them respond differently to pain and reduces its impact on their day.

Getting started

You may find it helpful to try short sessions regularly. Even a few minutes a day can make a difference over time.

  • Start with short, simple exercises
  • Find a quiet, comfortable space
  • Practice regularly, even on better days
  • Be patient while learning new techniques

Building the habit

Relaxation and mindfulness are skills that develop over time with practice.

It is common not to notice an immediate difference — this does not mean it is not working.

Practising regularly can help you build confidence and make these techniques more effective.

A balanced approach

Relaxation and mindfulness are one part of the wider Pain Management Jigsaw.

They work best alongside other approaches such as movement, pacing and goal setting.

Using a range of strategies together can help you feel more supported in managing pain.
