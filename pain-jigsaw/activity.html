Activity Management

Finding the right balance of activity and rest can help you stay active, reduce flare-ups and support steadier symptoms over time.

Finding the right balance

Many people living with persistent pain fall into a pattern of doing too much on a good day, followed by needing to rest for several days. This is sometimes called the “boom and bust” cycle.

Others may avoid activity because they are worried it will make pain worse. Both patterns can make pain harder to manage over time.

Activity management is about finding a steadier, more sustainable way of doing things.

Pacing your activity

Pacing means balancing activity and rest so you can stay active without increasing pain too much.

  • Break tasks into smaller steps
  • Take regular planned breaks
  • Stop before pain becomes much worse
  • Build activity gradually over time

Pacing can help you do more consistently, rather than doing too much and needing longer recovery afterwards.

Planning, pacing and prioritising

These simple strategies can help you manage your energy and activity more effectively:

  • Planning: Think ahead and build in rest breaks
  • Pacing: Spread tasks out and avoid doing too much at once
  • Prioritising: Focus on what matters most to you

Using these together can help you feel more in control of your day and reduce the impact of pain on daily life.

Making it work in daily life

  • Break household tasks into smaller stages
  • Alternate between activity and rest
  • Spread tasks across the week
  • Plan ahead for more demanding activities

Small changes in how you do things can make everyday tasks feel more manageable.

Building up gradually

When pain has affected your activity for a long time, it is normal to lose confidence as well as strength and stamina.

Building activity up gradually can help your body and mind adjust over time.

Progress does not have to be fast to be meaningful — small, steady steps are often the most sustainable.

A balanced approach

It is common to want to make the most of a good day, or to do less when pain feels difficult.

Neither response is “wrong” — but finding a steadier rhythm can often make pain easier to manage over time.

Activity management is not about doing everything or doing nothing. It is about finding a balance that supports your quality of life.
