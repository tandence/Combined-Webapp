Medication

Medication can be one part of managing pain. For some people, it can help improve day-to-day comfort and make it easier to do the things that matter to them. However, it is rarely a complete solution and works best when combined with other approaches.

Important things to know

  • Medication does not usually take pain away completely
  • Different people respond in different ways
  • For many people, medication may reduce pain by around 20–30%
  • The aim is often to improve daily function rather than remove pain completely
  • Medication works best alongside other approaches such as movement, pacing and support
  • Side effects and long-term risks need to be considered

Where medication fits

Pain management is often most effective when different approaches are used together. Medication is just one piece of the wider Pain Management Jigsaw.

Many other factors influence pain, including sleep, stress, activity levels, mood and daily routines. Focusing on these areas alongside medication can often make a bigger difference to how you feel day to day.

Using medication safely

Finding the right medication can take time. Your needs may change, so regular review is important.

  • Only take medication as prescribed
  • Do not take more than the recommended dose
  • Speak to a healthcare professional before making changes
  • Let your clinician know about any side effects
  • Ask questions if you are unsure about your medication

Types of medication

Different types of medication may be used depending on the nature of your pain:

  • Simple pain relief: e.g. paracetamol
  • Anti-inflammatory medicines: e.g. ibuprofen
  • Opioid medicines: e.g. tramadol, morphine
  • Other medicines: e.g. medications used for nerve pain, including some antidepressants

Some medications are not suitable for long-term use or require careful monitoring.

A balanced approach

Many people living with persistent pain have tried different medications over time. If your pain is still affecting you, this does not mean you have failed — it simply means that pain often needs to be managed in different ways.
