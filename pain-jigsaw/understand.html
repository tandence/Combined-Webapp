Understand Your Condition

Understanding pain can help you make sense of your experience, reduce worry, and feel more in control as you take steps towards living well with pain.

What is persistent pain?

Persistent pain is pain that continues for longer than expected, often beyond normal healing time.

It can behave differently from short-term pain and is influenced by many different factors, not just the body alone.

Why understanding your pain helps

Understanding how pain works can help reduce fear, build confidence and support you to try strategies that improve your quality of life.

  • Pain does not always mean harm or damage
  • Your nervous system can become more sensitive over time
  • Thoughts, emotions, sleep and activity all play a role
  • You can learn ways to reduce the impact of pain

Why pain can continue

Pain involves communication between the body and the brain. Even after tissues have healed, the brain can continue sending pain signals.

This does not mean the pain isn’t real — it reflects changes in how the nervous system is working.

Sometimes the system becomes over-sensitive, meaning it reacts more strongly to signals and keeps pain going.

What influences your pain

Pain is influenced by many different factors working together.

You can think of pain like a recipe — changing some of these “ingredients” can change your experience.

  • Sleep and energy levels
  • Thoughts and beliefs
  • Emotions and stress
  • Activity levels
  • Past experiences
  • Environment and support

Key things to know

  • Pain is always real, even if the cause is not clear
  • The body and brain work together in how pain is experienced
  • Persistent pain is a long-term condition
  • It can often be managed, even if it cannot be completely removed

A different way of thinking about pain

Many people expect pain to have a clear cause and a straightforward fix. While this works well for short-term problems, persistent pain often behaves differently.

Even when tests do not show a clear cause, your pain is still real. Looking at the whole picture — including the body, thoughts, emotions and daily life — can offer more helpful ways forward.

A balanced approach

Many people living with persistent pain have tried a range of treatments and supports.

If pain is still affecting you, this does not mean you have failed — it reflects how complex pain can be.

Understanding pain is often the first step towards finding approaches that work for you.
