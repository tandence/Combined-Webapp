Get Involved and Reconnect to Life

Staying connected to meaningful activities and people can support your wellbeing and help you live well alongside pain.

Why getting involved matters

Living with persistent pain can sometimes lead to doing less over time.

This can affect physical health, mood, confidence and your sense of identity.

Staying involved in meaningful activities can help improve how you feel both physically and emotionally.

Losing connection can happen gradually

Many people find that over time they begin to step back from activities, hobbies or social situations.

This can happen without you noticing at first, as you try to manage pain or avoid making it worse.

Reconnecting is about gently finding your way back to things that matter to you.

Starting small

Reconnecting does not have to mean making big changes.

Small, gradual steps can make a meaningful difference over time.

  • Start with activities you enjoy or value
  • Build activity up gradually
  • Focus on what matters to you
  • Be realistic and kind to yourself

Ways to reconnect

  • Join local support or interest groups
  • Try gentle physical activities
  • Reconnect with hobbies
  • Spend time with friends and family
  • Explore volunteering opportunities

There is no right or wrong way to reconnect — it is about finding what feels meaningful to you.

What matters to you

Reconnecting often starts with thinking about what is important in your life.

This might include relationships, hobbies, work, or everyday activities that give your day purpose.

Focusing on what matters can help make activities feel more meaningful and motivating.

Building confidence over time

It is normal to feel unsure about getting involved again after a period of doing less.

Confidence can build gradually as you take small steps and find what works for you.

Each step counts, no matter how small it feels.

A balanced approach

Reconnecting is one part of the wider Pain Management Jigsaw.

It works alongside movement, pacing, goals and managing thoughts and emotions.

Bringing these parts together can help support a more fulfilling and balanced life.
